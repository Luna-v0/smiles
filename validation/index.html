    Chemical Validation

    This document describes the chemistry validation algorithms used by the SMILES parser to ensure molecular structures are chemically valid.

    Valence policy (decided: permissive, octet-bound for row 2)

    The default chemistry backend (ChemistryValidator(backend="pysmiles")) is deliberately permissive: it accepts radicals and the expanded-octet / hypervalent species of period 3 and below — e.g. sulfate, [SH6], F[P-](F)(F)(F)(F)F — and rejects only physically impossible over-valence. This is a considered choice, not an omission:

    • OpenSMILES itself does not mandate a valence model; valence checking is a policy layered on top of the language.
    • Generative-model output (the primary use case) legitimately contains radicals and hypervalent species that RDKit's default sanitizer rejects.

    What counts as "impossible" (chem/adapter.py, valence_violations):

    Rule Example rejected Example accepted
    Second-row atoms (B, C, N, O, F) cannot expand their octet: only the lowest valence of the isoelectronic element (N 3, N⁺ 4, O 2, O⁻ 1) N(C)(C)(C)C, [NH4]C, [OH3]C, C(N)=[NH2] [NH4+], [NH2+]=C(N)N
    …except the conventional neutral pentavalent-N shorthands (RDKit charge-separates them) — CN(=O)=O, c1ccn(=O)cc1, C=N#N, N=N#N
    A closed-shell ion cannot bond [Cl-]C, [F-][B+3]… [Na+].[Cl-]
    d/f-block metals get no valence limit; a neutral N/O bonded to one is a dative donor [NH4][Pt]… [NH3][Pt]([NH3])(Cl)Cl, Cl[Pt-2](Cl)(Cl)Cl
    Main-group atoms of period 3+ use pysmiles' electron-count maximum O[AlH3](O)O O=S(=O)(O)O

    Until October 2026 neutral 4-valent N (N(C)(C)(C)C) and [OH4] were accepted as "hypervalent". Second-row atoms have no d orbitals to expand into, and public datasets showed these strings are always a missing charge or an extra hydrogen, so they are now rejected — with a repair suggestion.

    Repairs, body pH and salts

    A chemistry failure comes with a repair when changing one atom fixes it (chem/repair.py): drop hydrogens ([AlH3] → [Al]), add a charge (O=N([O-])C → O=[N+]([O-])C), or move a bonded ion's charge onto its partner ([Cl-][Pt] → Cl[Pt-]). Only the edited atoms are rewritten, so stereo and atom order are kept; ValidationResult.repaired_smiles holds the result and repairs one explanation per atom.

    When a nitrogen can be fixed either way (=[NH2] → =[NH2+] or =[NH]), physiological_ph=True (default) picks its protonation state at pH 7.4 from a small pKa table (PKA_TABLE: guanidines and amidines protonated, N-cyano guanidines neutral, …). With it off, the written charge is kept.

    remove_salts=True (default) splits multi-component input into parent_smiles (most heavy atoms) and removed_fragments. Every component is still validated: a broken counter-ion makes the record invalid and is named in failed_fragment, so salt removal never hides a data error. Because the repair is computed over the whole molecule rather than per fragment, a multi-component record can have both failed_fragment and repaired_smiles set at once: repaired_smiles is the full repaired input (every component, not just the one that failed), and failed_fragment still names which original component made the record invalid as written.

    Closed-shell check (opt-in)

    closed_shell=True (default off) adds a second, independent check that never touches valid: it warns about every atom left with an odd number of unpaired electrons — an open shell, i.e. a radical — and, when the molecule is otherwise chemistry-valid, suggests the edit that closes it.

    This fills a real gap in the permissive valence policy above: that policy only rejects over-valence, so a missing hydrogen or a missing negative charge — which move an atom to under-valence — pass through silently today (confirmed on the known-answer corruption test: analyses/feature_eval.py corrupt, ~1% detected before this option, close to 100% with it on; see the 2026-10-17 report). closed_shell is how a caller who cares about that gap opts into catching it, without changing what the validator accepts by default.

    • Warning: one line per open-shell atom in ValidationResult.warnings, e.g. "O (atom 3) is open-shell: 1 unpaired electron (a radical; accepted under the permissive valence policy)".
    • Repair: when the molecule is otherwise valid, repaired_smiles and repairs hold the shell-closing edit — add the missing hydrogen(s), or add a matching negative charge. physiological_ph (the same flag the nitrogen repair above uses) picks between them for an open-shell oxygen, by a small pKa table of its own (chem/repair.py: O_PKA_TABLE): carboxylate and nitro/nitroso O are deprotonated (so a negative charge is added) at pH 7.4, phenolic and aliphatic-alcohol O are protonated (so a hydrogen is added) — the same reasoning PKA_TABLE already applies to nitrogen. For every other element, or with physiological_ph=False, a hydrogen is preferred when one closes the shell. If the molecule is invalid for an unrelated reason (a real over-valence failure elsewhere), the warning is still reported but no closed-shell repair is suggested — repaired_smiles keeps meaning "this makes the whole molecule valid," not a mix of two unrelated fixes.
    • Never flags: a lone pair is not a radical. An atom's shortfall against its ground-state valence only forces an unpaired electron when that shortfall is odd (one electron with no partner); an even shortfall (e.g. a singlet carbene) can pair up on its own and is not reported — the simple electron-count model used here cannot tell that apart from a triplet biradical, and guessing would be worse than saying nothing.
    • Exempt, matching the valence policy: d/f-block metals (no simple valence model applies), wildcard atoms, free (disconnected) ions, and closed-shell ions. Aromatic rings are handled correctly — the same aromaticity perception and pi-bond accounting the valence policy uses means a normal aromatic ring never reports a false radical. A main-group metal with a genuine unpaired electron (e.g. bare [Na]) is not exempt: it is already accepted today as a radical under the permissive policy, and this check names that same radical.
    • Explicit radical notation ([CH3], nitroxide R2N[O], …) is reported as a warning, never an error — it already validates today and keeps doing so; closed_shell only makes the radical visible.

    cis/trans marks

    / and \ mark cis/trans direction. OpenSMILES spells them as directional single bonds, so C=N\#N is two bonds in a row and a syntax error there. This validator treats the mark as an annotation instead: written in front of another bond symbol it is accepted, ignored (a double or triple bond carries no cis/trans direction of its own) and reported in ValidationResult.warnings.

    Callers who want RDKit-equivalent strictness can opt in with ChemistryValidator(backend="rdkit") / validate_smiles_detailed(mol, backend="rdkit").

    Note that C11 — which the pysmiles backend used to wave through — is now rejected before chemistry, by the ring-semantics tier (OpenSMILES §3.4: an atom cannot close a ring onto itself). Ring-closure rules are language semantics, not valence policy.

    Pipeline

    validate_smiles_detailed runs four tiers in order (validate_smiles wraps it and returns the (is_valid, exception) tuple). The first tier that rejects is reported in ValidationResult.tier:

    1. lex: the SLY lexer tokenises the string.
    2. grammar: the LALR(1) parser checks OpenSMILES syntax.
    3. ring_semantics: ParserManager enforces the ring-closure rules of OpenSMILES §3.4/§3.6 while it builds the molecular graph.
    4. chemistry: chem.adapter.to_descriptor turns the graph into a backend-neutral atom and bond list, and ChemistryValidator hands it to the chosen backend. The backend never sees the SMILES string.
      • pysmiles (default): aromaticity perception and kekulization (correct_aromatic_rings(strict=True), which fails on a region marked aromatic that cannot be kekulized), hydrogen filling, then the valence policy above (valence_violations).
      • rdkit: Chem.SanitizeMol on a molecule built atom by atom.

    After the chemistry tier, a failure gets a repair suggestion and a multi-component input is split into parent and counter-ions, as described above. cis/trans warnings are collected for every input that parses.

    Legacy validator

    Until October 2026 a hand-written validator applied the octet rule to acyclic bracket atoms and Hückel's 4n+2 rule to aromatic cycles. That code has been removed; its algorithms are kept for reference on Legacy Validation Algorithms.
